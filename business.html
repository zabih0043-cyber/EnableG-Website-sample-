Know what a job is worth before you say yes.

Most small operators lose money on the quote, not on the work. Get Paid counts the materials, the transport and your own hours, tells you whether the job is worth taking, and turns the quote into an invoice when it is done.

Price a job
Step 1

Price it properly

Materials, transport, and the hours it will actually take, including yours. The number at the bottom is what the job costs you before you have made anything.

Step 2

Check it is worth taking

It shows you what you would clear and what you would earn an hour. Some jobs are better turned down, and it is cheaper to find that out now than halfway through.

Step 3

Send it and get paid

A quote your customer can read, which becomes an invoice when the work is finished. Works with no signal, so a site with bad reception is not a problem.

Why this matters

The quote is where the money is usually lost.

Underpricing rarely feels like a mistake at the time. It feels like winning the job. The cost shows up later, in the hours you did not count and the trips you forgot about.

1

Count your own hours. Working for nothing is still working.

2

Count the travel. Three trips to a supplier is half a day.

3

Put it in writing. A quote on paper is harder to argue with later than one agreed over the phone.
