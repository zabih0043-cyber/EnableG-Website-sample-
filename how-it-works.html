How It Works

Ten minutes now, ten minutes in six weeks.

There is no sign-up and no waiting. You open a tool, answer honestly, and get one thing to do. The second reading is the one that actually tells you something, so the whole point is coming back.

Open a tool
The four steps

Nobody has to speak to anyone.

You can do all four on a phone, on your own, without telling us who you are. If you want to talk to someone afterwards you can, but nothing here requires it.

1

Pick one

Whichever question is actually bothering you. There is a suggested order and you are free to ignore it.

2

Answer honestly

Four to ten minutes. Nothing is sent anywhere, so the honest answer costs you nothing and is the only useful one.

3

Do one thing

You get a result and three suggestions. Pick one. Doing a single one beats agreeing with all three.

4

Come back

In about six weeks, run the same tool again. The difference between the two readings is the actual result.

Why so short

A tool you finish beats a better one you abandon.

Nothing here takes longer than ten minutes, and that is a design decision rather than a limitation. A forty-question assessment gets abandoned at question twelve, usually by the people who most needed the answer.

1

Short enough to finish in one sitting, on a taxi or a lunch break.

2

Blunt questions, because polite ones produce polite answers that tell you nothing.

3

One suggested action at the end, not a report you have to work through.

If you stop halfway

Your answers stay on the device, so you can close it and pick up where you left off.

A thoughtful process for people who want progress with more calm, clarity, and structure.
